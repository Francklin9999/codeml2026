## 1. Context and evidence

The rubric gives points for a jury member opening the deliverable and finding proof. Strategy 7 proposes precise deep links, while strategy 12 simulates evaluators. No ledger, complete Q&A, or jury benchmark has been completed; the proposal tests whether a person can actually retrieve a source without coaching.

## 2. Idea and novelty

Recruit two teammates who did not curate the ledger. Give each five operational tasks drawn from the 10 questions and handoff brief. Observe time, clicks, wrong-source choices, and whether they distinguish proposal, decision, validation, and stale document. Unlike click-count optimization in strategy 7, this measures successful task completion and interpretation by a cold user.

## 3. Rubric

An understandable evidence path supports the jury's own verification and demonstrates practical takeover value.

## 4. Implementation

Create `work/strat21/tasks.md`, anonymized observation sheets, and a findings summary. Prewrite tasks and success criteria before testing; no hints during each attempt. Use only corpus-grounded answers and record locator failures for correction.

## 5. Experiment and decision

Baseline: current static or draft navigation. Test 10 tasks per person. Proposed adoption: at least 9/10 tasks end at the correct source and correct status classification within 90 seconds; revise if any task directs a user to stale support. Do not claim this predicts jury score.

## 6. Risks

Teammates may be more familiar than actual evaluators; small task sample and shared language can mask usability gaps.

## 7. Combines with

Strategies 1, 7, 9, 12; rerun after material navigation changes.
