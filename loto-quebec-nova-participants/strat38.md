## 1. Context and evidence

The rubric requests a one-page takeover brief, and strategy 9 drafts it. Compression can omit conditional status, remaining conditions, budget nuance, or invoice distinctions. This proposal tests the brief as a usable summary rather than scoring it with another simulated jury.

## 2. Idea and novelty

Give an uninvolved reader 60 seconds to read the brief, then remove it and ask them to recall owner, approved date and caveat, current blocker/conditions, budget status, and first next action. Unlike strategy 12's multi-persona rubric simulation, this measures retention of key operational facts after brief exposure.

## 3. Rubric

A concise brief succeeds only if a new operator retains the essential state and does not remember recommendations as commitments.

## 4. Implementation

Create `work/strat38/recall_test.md` with five target facts predeclared from verified sources. Test three readers separately and record exact recall, confidence, and mistaken assumptions. Change the brief only after reviewing source provenance.

## 5. Experiment and decision

Baseline: current brief. Proposed adoption: all three readers recall conditional date and at least four of five target facts; zero readers treat proposed actions as approved commitments. If failed, shorten secondary context and make caveats visually prominent; rerun with new readers.

## 6. Risks

Small test sample and short-term recall do not measure full usability. Repeated exposure can bias retest; use fresh readers after revisions.

## 7. Combines with

Strategies 9, 21, 33; keep recall targets grounded in verified claims.
